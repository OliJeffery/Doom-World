# Character Creation

Making Death World: Catachan characters is quick and easy, for life is cheap and death is constant. You should all create your first characters together at the beginning of your first session. Character creation is, just like play, a kind of conversation—everyone should be there for it, for the cohesion you build in creation will be tested in the crucible of endless horror.

You may need to make another character during play, if yours gets killed for example. On Catachan, death comes quickly and often, and the jungle cares nothing for your plans or desires. If so, no worries, the character creation process helps you make a new character that fits into the squad in just a few minutes, a replacement for the fallen, another soul to throw into the endless struggle. All characters, even replacement characters, start at first level, for experience must be earned in blood and suffering.

Most everything you need to create a character you will find on the character sheets. These steps will walk you through filling out a character sheet, through the process of creating a soul that will be tested by the endless horror of the Death World.

## 1. Choose a Specialization

Look over the character specializations and choose one that interests you. To start with everyone chooses a different specialization; there aren't two snipers. If two people want the same specialization, talk it over like adults and compromise.

I sit down with Dan, Rob, Tom, and Kris to play a game run by Oli. I've got some cool ideas for a scout, so I mention that would be my first choice. No one else was thinking of playing one, so I take the scout character sheet.

## 2. Choose Your Regiment

All Catachan Jungle Fighters are human, but different regiments have different traditions and specialties. Choose your regiment. Your regiment gives you a special move.

I like the idea of being part of a regiment known for stealth—the Unseen Lurkers sound perfect. They wear dark fatigues and paint their wargear black, which fits my scout character perfectly.

## 3. Choose a Name

Choose your character's name from the list.

Rock sounds good—tough and simple, like a Catachan should be.

## 4. Choose Look

Your look is your physical appearance. Choose one item from each list.

Hard eyes sound good since every Catachan has seen things that would break lesser men, horrors that should not exist, darkness that hungers for the soul. No time for hair styling in the jungle so shorn hair it is. My fatigues are tattered and patched, covered in mud and blood from countless missions, each stain a testament to the ceaseless battle. Years of jungle survival have left me with a scarred body covered in the marks of close calls with death, each scar a prayer to the Emperor that I survived.

## 5. Choose Stats

Assign these scores to your stats: 16, 15, 13, 12, 10, 10, 9, 8, 8. Start by looking over the basic moves and the starting moves for your specialization. Pick out the move that interests you the most: something you'll be doing a lot, or something that you excel at. Put a 16 in the stat for that move. Look over the list again and pick out the next most important move to your character, maybe something that supports your first choice. Put your 15 in the stat for that move. Repeat this process for your remaining scores: 13, 12, 10, 10, 9, 8, 8. You have nine stats total: Weapon Skill, Ballistic Skill, Strength, Toughness, Agility, Intelligence, Perception, Willpower, and Leadership. Make sure every stat gets a score.

It looks like I need Perception to track and scout, which is my thing, so my 16 goes there. The press on option for Agility looks like something I might be doing to dive out of the way of a Catachan Devil's claws, so that gets my 15. A 13 Toughness will help me survive the jungle's many poisons and diseases. Intelligence might be useful in identifying threats and reading the signs, so I'll put my 12 there. Weapon Skill gets a 10 since I might need to fight up close. Ballistic Skill gets a 10 for ranged combat. Strength gets my 9 for some extra damage. Willpower gets an 8—I'm tough but not unbreakable. Leadership gets the last 8—I'm not here to make friends, I'm here to survive.

## 7. Set Maximum Wounds

Your maximum Wounds is equal to your class's base Wounds+Toughness score. You start with your maximum Wounds.

Base 4 plus 9 Toughness gives me a whopping 13 Wounds.

## 8. Choose Starting Moves

The front side of each character sheet lists the starting moves. Some specializations, like the heavy weapons specialist, have choices to make as part of one of their moves. Make these choices now.

As a scout, I take the Hunt and Track move since that's what I do. I also take Camouflage since staying hidden is how I survive. I choose my signature weapon—a Catachan knife that's been with me through countless missions.

## 9. Choose Your Duty

Your duty is a few words that describe what drives your character. Each specialization may only start with certain duties. Choose your duty—in play, it'll give your character certain actions that can earn you additional XP

The Survival option for scouts says I earn extra XP when I help the squad avoid a deadly threat. Rock is all about keeping everyone alive—I'll go with Survival.

## 10. Choose Gear

Each specialization has choices to make for starting gear. Keep your load in mind—it limits how much you can easily carry. Make sure to total up your armor and note it on your character sheet.

I'm worried about my Wounds, so I take flak armor. My Catachan knife is my primary weapon, but I also take a lascarbine for ranged combat. It's a toss-up between the medkit and the antitoxin, but the medkit wins out since I'm the one who patches people up. I also end up with some rations and a canteen.

## 11. Introduce Your Character

Now that you know who your character is, it's time to introduce them to everyone else. Wait until everyone's finished choosing their name. Then go around the table; when it's your turn, share your look, specialization and anything else pertinent about your character. You can share your duty now or keep it a secret if you prefer.

This is also the time for the GM to ask questions. The GM's questions should help establish the relationships between characters ("What do you think about that?") and draw the squad into the mission ("Does that mean you've served with Sergeant Jackson before?"). The GM should listen to everything in the description and ask about anything that stands out. Establish which regiment they're from, who they are, how they came together as a squad, or anything else that seems relevant or interesting.

"This is Rock, scout from the Catachan III 'Green Devils'! He's got hard eyes, shorn hair, tattered fatigues covered in mud and blood, and a scarred body. He's been on Catachan his whole life and knows the jungle better than most men know their own homes."

## 12. Establish Squad Cohesion

Once everyone has described their characters, discuss how your squad came together. What missions have you survived together? How long have you been a unit? What shared experiences bind you together? The GM should ask questions about your history together, your relationships, and what makes you trust each other—or what makes you wary.

Squad Cohesion starts at 1. It represents how well your squad works together as a unit. When you make the Aid or Interfere move, you roll+Squad Cohesion. Take some time to discuss your squad's history and let the GM ask questions about how you came together, what you've survived, and what challenges you've faced as a unit.

With everyone introduced, we discuss how our squad came together. Dan's close combat specialist Marcus and Kris's medic Doc have been serving together for months, surviving the fall of Outpost Gamma-7. Rob's scout Kael joined the squad after his previous unit was wiped out by Catachan Devils. Tom's heavy weapons specialist Jax is the newest member, transferred in after the last heavy weapons specialist died. We've been together for three missions now, and while we're not a perfect unit, we've learned to watch each other's backs. Our Squad Cohesion starts at 1.

## 13. Get Ready to Play

Take a little break: grab a drink, stretch your legs and let the GM brainstorm for a little bit about what they've learned about your characters. Once you're all ready, grab your dice and your sheet and get ready to face the jungle.

Once you're ready the GM will get things started as described in the First Session chapter.

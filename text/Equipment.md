# Equipment

The abandoned outposts and forgotten caches of Catachan are filled with useful gear, the remnants of those who came before, the tools of the fallen, the weapons of the dead. The close combat specialist can find a well-maintained chainsword, its teeth still sharp, its spirit still hungry for xenos blood. The sniper might stumble across special ammunition, rounds blessed by the Omnissiah, each one a prayer to the Emperor. Most items are mundane—not relics or intrinsically unique in any way, but tools of war that have served the Imperium's will. Any item that's a relic or one-of-a-kind isn't mundane for the purposes of moves, for such items carry the weight of history and the blessing of the Emperor. A specialist's signature weapon is never mundane, for it's an extension of their will, a tool of the Emperor's will made manifest.

Each piece of equipment will have a number of tags. These will tell you something about how the equipment affects the character using it (like +Armor) or suggest something about the way it is used (like the Range tags). Like everything else in Death World: Catachan, these guide the fiction you are creating in play, each tag a reflection of the harsh realities of survival in the endless darkness. If a weapon is awkward, it might mean that you are more likely to drop it when you fail that close combat roll, when the darkness closes in and death itself reaches for you.

By no means is this an exhaustive list—feel free to create your own tags, to give form to the unending terror through the tools of war that the characters use to survive.

### General Equipment Tags

These are general tags that can apply to just about any piece of gear. You’ll see them on armor, weapons or general adventuring tools.

*Applied*: It’s only useful when carefully applied to a person or to something they eat or drink.

*Awkward*: It’s unwieldy and tough to use.

*+Bonus*: It modifies your effectiveness in a specified situation. It might be "Advantage to Jungle Knowledge" or "Disadvantage to close combat."

*n Respect*: How much respect is required to requisition this item. Respect is earned through service, successful missions, and standing within the Imperial Guard. If the cost includes "-Leadership" a little negotiation or calling in favors subtracts the requester's Leadership score (not modifier) from the respect required.

*Standard Issue*: This item is automatically assigned to all Imperial Guard personnel. No respect required.

*Dangerous*: It’s easy to get in trouble with it. If you interact with it without proper precautions the GM may freely invoke the consequences of your foolish actions.

*Ration*: It’s edible, more or less.

*Requires*: It’s only useful to certain people. If you don’t meet the requirements it works poorly, if at all.

*Slow*: It takes minutes or more to use.

*Touch*: It’s used by touching it to the target’s skin.

*Two-handed*: It takes two hands to use it effectively.

*n weight*: Count the listed amount against your load. Something with no listed weight isn't designed to be carried. 100 thrones worth of trade goods is 1 weight. The same value in gems or fine art may be lighter or heavier.

*Worn*: To use it, you have to be wearing it.

*n Uses*: It can only be used*n*times.

## Weapons

Each weapon has a damage die that represents its base lethality, but a weapon's true value lies in its tags which describe what the weapon is useful for, how it serves the Emperor's will. A weapon is only as deadly as the warrior who wields it—faith in the Emperor, the will to survive, and the fury to face the darkness are what truly bring death to the enemies of mankind. A Catachan knife isn't useful because it does more or less damage than some other blade. It's useful because it's small and easy to strike with at close distance, because it is an extension of the wielder's will, a tool of death in the ceaseless battle. A lasgun in the hands of a green recruit isn't nearly so dangerous as one in the hands of a skilled veteran, for experience is the only teacher that matters on a Death World, and every lesson is learned in blood.

### Weapon Tags

Weapons may have tags that are primarily there to help you describe them (like Rusty or Glowing) but these tags have a specific, mechanical effect.

*n Ammo*: It counts as ammunition for appropriate ranged weapons. The number indicated does not represent individual rounds or clips, but represents what you have left on hand.

*Forceful*: It can knock someone back a pace, maybe even off their feet.

*+n Damage*: It's particularly harmful to your enemies. When you deal damage, you add*n*to it.

*Ignores Armor*: Don’t subtract armor from the damage taken.

*Messy*: It does damage in a particularly destructive way, ripping people and things apart.

*n Piercing*: It goes right through armor. When you deal damage with*n*piercing, you subtract*n*from the enemy’s armor for that attack.

*Precise*: It rewards careful strikes. You use Agility for close combat with this weapon, not Weapon Skill.

*Reload*: After you attack with it, it takes more than a moment to reset for another attack.

*Stun*: When you attack with it, it does stun damage instead of normal damage.

*Thrown*: Throw it at someone to hurt them. If you volley with this weapon, you can't choose to mark off ammo on a 7–9; once you throw it, it's gone until you can recover it.

*Rapid fire*: When you attack with a rapid fire weapon, you may choose to fire multiple shots. For each additional shot beyond the first, reduce your ammo by 1 and deal +1d4 damage. You may fire up to 3 shots in a single attack. On a 7–9, if you fired multiple shots, you must also choose one of the normal 7–9 options in addition to the ammo cost.

Weapons have tags to indicate the range at which they are useful. Death World: Catachan doesn’t inflict penalties or grant bonuses for “optimal range” or the like, but if your weapon says Hand and an enemy's ten yards away, a player would have a hard time justifying using that weapon against him.

*Hand*: It’s useful for attacking something within your reach, no further.

*Close*: It’s useful for attacking something at arm’s reach plus a foot or two.

*Reach*: It’s useful for attacking something that’s several feet away—maybe as far as ten.

*Near*: It’s useful for attacking if you can see the whites of their eyes.

*Far*: It’s useful for attacking something in shouting distance.

## Armor

Armor is heavy, difficult to wear and is damned uncomfortable in the Catachan heat. Some specializations are better trained to ignore these drawbacks, but anyone can strap on a suit of armor and enjoy the benefits it grants.

### Armor Tags

Armor, like weapons, has tags. Some are purely descriptive but the ones below have some mechanical effect on the player wearing them

*n Armor*: It protects you from harm and absorbs damage. When you take damage, subtract your armor from the total. If you have more than one item with*n Armor*, only the highest value counts.

*+n Armor*: It protects you and stacks with other armor. Add its value to your total armor.

*Clumsy*: It's tough to move around with. Disadvantage while using it. This penalty is cumulative.

## Field Gear

Field Gear	5 uses, 2 Respect, 1 weight

Field gear is a collection of useful mundane items such as rope, spikes, markers, tools, etc. When you rummage through your field gear for some useful mundane item, you find what you need and mark off a use.

Field Dressings	3 uses, slow, standard issue, 0 weight

When you have a few minutes to bandage someone else's wounds, heal them of 4 damage and expend a use.

Medkit	2 uses, slow, 3 Respect, 1 weight

When you carefully treat someone's wounds with a medkit, heal them of 7 damage and expend a use.

Stim-Pack	1 use, 5 Respect, 0 weight

When you inject a stim-pack, heal yourself of 10 damage or remove one debility, your choice.

Jungle Remedies	2 uses, slow, 2 Respect, 1 weight

When you carefully treat someone's wounds with jungle remedies, heal them of 6 damage and expend a use. These remedies are made from Catachan plants and are more effective against local toxins.

Rations	Ration, 5 uses, standard issue, 1 weight

Standard Imperial Guard rations. Not tasty, but not bad either. Keeps you alive.

Jungle Rations	Ration, 7 uses, 2 Respect, 1 weight

Special rations made from Catachan flora and fauna. Only the most experienced jungle fighters know how to prepare them safely.

Antitoxin	1 use, 2 Respect, 0 weight

When you inject antitoxin, you're cured of one poison or toxin affecting you.

Catachan Tobacco	6 uses, 1 Respect, 0 weight

When you share Catachan tobacco with someone, expend two uses and take Advantage to intimidate or negotiate with them.

Auspex Scanner	3 uses, 4 Respect, 1 weight

When you use an auspex scanner to detect threats or hidden objects, mark off a use and take +1 to read the jungle or discern realities.

Vox-Caster	1 use, 3 Respect, 2 weight

When you use a vox-caster to communicate with your outpost or other squads, mark off a use. The jungle's interference makes long-range communication difficult.

# Relics and Ancient Technology

There are stranger things on Catachan than lasguns and flak armor. Relics and ancient technology are items that have intrinsic power beyond the mundane.

Relics are for you to make for your game. Players may discover relics through successful missions, ancient caches, or as rewards for exceptional service. The GM can introduce relics in the spoils of battle or the rewards for completing dangerous missions. This list provides some ideas, but relics are ultimately for you to decide.

When making your own relics keep in mind that these items are*exceptional*. Simple modifiers, like +1 damage, are the realm of the mundane—relics should provide more interesting bonuses and unique capabilities.

The Emperor's Blade	Close, 2 weight

There are many chainswords in the galaxy, but there is only one Emperor's Blade. It's a weapon of ancient design, revered as a holy relic by all who serve the God-Emperor. Its touch is a blessing and to many, the sight of it brings tears of joy.

In the hands of a sergeant, it strikes true and strong. A sergeant wielding it increases their damage die to d12 and has access to every sergeant move. As well, The Emperor's Blade can harm any creature of Chaos or Xenos, regardless of any defenses it may have. No creature of the Enemy may touch it without suffering agony. In the hands of any non-sergeant, it's merely a chainsword, heavier and more cumbersome than most—it gains the awkward tag.

The Emperor's Blade, while not intelligent, will forever be drawn to a cause of true Duty, like iron to a magnet.

Seeker Rounds	1 ammo, 1 weight

Crafted by the Tech-Priests of Mars using ancient targeting systems, these special rounds can find their target in even the deepest darkness. A soldier may fire them blind, in the dark, with their eyes bound by heavy cloth and still be assured of a clean shot. The rounds contain micro-servitors that guide them to their target. If exposed to the light of a star, however, the delicate guidance systems overload and the rounds become useless.

Power Axe of the Regimental Hero	Close, 1 weight

It is crafted of adamantium, glowing with a golden light and imbued with the authority of the Emperor. When you bear the axe, you become a beacon of inspiration to all you lead. Any soldiers under your command have +1 Loyalty, no matter the quality of your leadership.

Sanctified Spike	0 weight

A nail or spike, twisted and forever cold, said to have been blessed by the Ecclesiarchy and sanctified against the Warp. When hammered into a corpse, it disappears and ensures that corpse will never be risen again—no Warp power short of that of the Chaos Gods themselves can reignite the flame of life (natural or otherwise) in the body.

Stasis Field Generator	0 weight

A stasis field generator is larger on the inside than the outside, it can contain an infinite number of items, and its weight never increases. The field freezes items in time, allowing for compact storage. When you try to retrieve an item from a stasis field generator, roll+Intelligence. ✴On a 10+, it's right there. ✴On a 7-9, choose one:

- You get the exact item, but it takes a while
- You get a similar item of the GM's choice, but it only takes a moment

No matter how many items it contains, a stasis field generator is always 0 weight.

Ration Dispenser	1 weight

A brass vox-horn, curled and ornate, carved with symbols of the Imperial Aquila. When activated, in addition to sound, the device dispenses rations. Enough to feed a meal to everyone who hears its activation signal.

The Carcosan Spire	Reach, Thrown, 3 weight

None know from whence this spear of twisted white material comes. Those who bear it too long find their minds full of alien dreams and begin to hear the strange thoughts of the Warp. None are impervious. Used against any "natural" target (humans, Orks, mutants and the like) the Spire acts as a mere mortal spear. Its true purpose is to do harm to those things whose strange natures protect them against mundane weapons. Used thus, the Spire can wound foes otherwise invulnerable to harm. The wielder will recognize these twisted foes on sight—the Spire knows its own.

Data-Slate with Tech-Ritual	1 use, 0 weight

A data-slate has a tech-ritual inscribed on it. The ritual must be performable by you or within your specialization's capabilities for you to be able to perform it. When you perform a ritual from a data-slate, the ritual takes effect, simple as that.

Sanctified Oil	1 use, 0 weight

A holy oil, created in limited supply by a mute sect of Ecclesiarchy priests whose order protected humanity from the powers of the Warp in ancient epochs. Only a few jars remain. When applied to any weapon and used to strike a denizen of the Warp, the oil undoes the power that binds that creature. In some cases, this will return it to the Warp. In others, it merely undoes any Warp power controlling it. The oil stays on the weapon for a few hours before it dries and flakes away.

If applied to the edges of a doorway or drawn in a circle, the oil will repel creatures whose home is the Warp. They can't pass across it. The oil lasts for one full day before it soaks in or evaporates.

Truth Serum	1 use, 0 weight

A yellowish chemical compound. Seems never to run out and the light it casts is strange and weak. Its consistency remains constant, too. Inject the serum into a target and gain 3 hold. Spend that hold and ask your target a question. They find themselves telling you the whole truth, despite themselves. The consequences, after the fact? Those are up to you to deal with.

The Epoch Lens	1 weight

A Tech-Priest, old and too frail to leave his forge, crafted this intricate and fragile device of glass and gold to examine the histories and relics he so loved. Looking at an object through the lens reveals visions of who made it and where it came from.

The Fiasco Codex	0 weight

A thick tome, said to be penned in the blood of poor fools and heretics by some daemon prince possessed of dark humor, this tome details tales and stories of those whose ambition overwhelmed their reason. Reading from this tome teaches one the value of clear-headedness but leaves a sense of dread behind. When you read from the Fiasco Codex, Roll+Willpower. ✴On a 10+, ask two of the questions below. ✴On a 7–9, ask one.

- What's my greatest opportunity, right now?
- Who can I betray to gain an advantage?
- Who's an ally I shouldn't trust?

The codex gives up its answers only once to each reader and takes 2 to 3 hours to read.

Rebreather Unit	0 weight

A simple thing, but useful when you need a breath of fresh air. The unit appears empty but can't be filled, anything added to it simply spills out. This is because the unit is eternally full of air. If placed underwater, it will bubble forever. If pressed to the mouth, one can breathe normally—toxic fumes are no concern, for example. I'm sure you'll find all sorts of unusual uses for it.

Jump Pack	1 weight

Who hasn't always wanted to soar the pretty blue sky? In an attempt to grant the wishes of land-bound soldiers, these great jump packs were created. Known by many names and crafted by as many Tech-Priests, they commonly take the shape of whatever local design patterns hold favor. Worn by means of a harness or, in some dire cases, a surgical procedure.

When you take to the air with this jump pack, roll+Agility. ✴On a 10+, your flight is controlled and you may stay aloft as long as you like. ✴On a 7–9, you make it aloft but your flight is short or erratic and unpredictable, your choice. ✴On a 6-, you make it aloft, but the coming-down part and everything between is up to the GM.

Auspex Goggles	0 weight

Rough-hewn lenses in metal frames. Dinged up and barely held together, they somehow allow the wearer to see much more than their naked eyes might. When you read the signs wearing these gifted lenses, you get to bend the rules a little. On a roll of 10+, ask any three questions you like. They don't have to be on the list. As long as sight could give you answers, the GM will tell you what you want to know.

The Ku’meh Maneuver	1 weight

A great, leathery tome worn shiny by the hands of a hundred great generals, this book is often passed from warrior to warrior, from father to son along the great battle lines that have divided Death World: Catachan’s past. Anyone reading it may, upon finishing for the first time, roll+Intelligence. ✴On a 10+, hold 3. ✴On a 7-9, hold 1. You may spend your hold to advise a companion on some matter of strategic or tactical significance. This advice allows you to, at any time, regardless of distance, roll to aid them on any one roll. On a miss, the GM can hold 1 and spend it to apply -2 to any roll of yours or the poor sap who listened to your advice.

Lamented Memento	0 weight

Taking the form of a single lock of bright red hair, bound in a black ribbon and immune to the ravages of time, the Lamented Memento bears a grim enchantment. In it are the memories and emotions of a girl who dealt with Death at the Black Gates so many times that, in the end, they fell in love and she left the world to be with him for a time. Her memory protects the wielder. If he finds himself at the Gates, the Memento can be traded for an automatic result of 10+ on the At Death's Door move.

Lodestone Shield	+1 armor, 1 weight

What mixed-up Tech-Priest made this? Shields are meant to repel metal, not draw it in! Emblazoned with an Imperial Aquila, the Lodestone Shield has the power to pull blades and projectiles to it. When you provide cover fire against enemies using metal weapons you can spend one hold, per target, to disarm them. Also, sometimes you'll find a handful of loose thrones stuck to it.

Servitor Skull	1 weight

An old skull, missing its jaw and very much worse-for-wear. The skull remembers the folly of its former owner—a man with more honor than sense. Once per night, the owner of the skull can ask "Who has it in for me?" and the skull will give up one name in a sad, lonely voice. If the owner of the skull is ever killed, it disappears surreptitiously. No one knows where it might turn up next.

Sacred Herbs	0 weight

The sacred herbs, collected and prepared by an order of lost Ecclesiarchy monks, can be found in bundles with two or three uses to them. Kept dry, they last indefinitely. When smoked in a pipe or consumed in an incense burner and the thick, blue smoke inhaled, these herbs will grant you strange visions of faraway places and distant times. If you focus your will on a particular person, place or thing, the herbs will respond: roll+Intelligence. ✴On a 10+, the vision is clear and useful—yielding some valid information. ✴On a 7–9, the vision is about the thing desired, but is unclear, fraught with metaphor or somehow difficult to understand. ✴On a miss, the GM will ask you, "What's it you fear most?" You must answer honestly, of course.

Teleportation Chamber	Slow

James Ninefingers, eccentric genius Tech-Priest, created these room-sized technological apparati. A metal chamber etched with runes and circuitry, glowing with a faint blue light. When you enter and say aloud the name of a location, roll+Intelligence. ✴On a 10+, you arrive exactly where you'd intended. ✴On a 7–9, the GM chooses a safe location nearby. ✴On a miss, you end up someplace. Maybe it's nearby? It's definitely not safe. Strange things sometimes happen to those who bend time and space with these devices.

Assassin's Armor	1 armor, 1 weight

A stealthy suit of armor, crafted by the Officio Assassinorum or their agents. It appears as many things to many people and blends in with appropriate apparel through advanced chameleonic technology. The wearer always seems the height of Imperial fashion to any who gaze upon him, and can move through crowds unnoticed.

The Sterling Hand	0 weight

Crafted by Tech-Priests, this mirrored-metal hand is deeply scored with runes of power and rejuvenation. Meant to replace wounded or destroyed limbs from combat accidents, the Sterling Hand bonds to the wound, old or new, and is strong and stout. It can be used as a weapon (Near range) and is made of pure enough silver to harm creatures affected by such.

Violation Glaive	Reach, 2 weight

A legendary blade, said to have been thrust backwards in time from some grim future, the violation glaive is crafted of strange green iron. The blade strikes at the mind of those it wounds, as well as the body. When you engage in close combat on a 10+ you have an additional option: you can deal your normal damage, let them counterattack you, and instill the emotion of your choice (maybe fear, reverence, or trust).

Vorpal Chainsword	Close, 3 piercing, 2 weight

Snicker-snack and all that. Sharp as anything, this simple-seeming chainsword means to separate one thing from another—the limb from the body or folk from their lives. When you deal damage with the Vorpal Chainsword, your enemy must choose something (an item, an advantage, a limb) and lose it, permanently.

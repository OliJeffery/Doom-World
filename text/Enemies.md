# Terrain

Great soldiers need horrendous antagonists, abominations that challenge their devotion, horrors that defy nature. This section is about how to create and play as those antagonists—from the lowly grot to the hellish Catachan Devil, from the xenos filth that hungers for human flesh to the Warp-touched abominations that defy comprehension.

## Using Monsters

A monster is any living (or unnatural) thing that stands in the characters' way, any abomination that hungers for their death, any horror that should not exist but does, defying the natural order with its very presence.

How you use these monsters follows directly from your agenda and principles. Stay true to your principles, use your moves and pursue your agenda—you cannot go wrong, for the darkness is endless and the horrors are infinite.

Your first agenda is to*portray a grim world*. The way you describe the monsters and adversaries the characters face can be a tool to help you fulfill that agenda, to manifest the unending terror of the Death World. Describing those creatures and people in vivid detail will bring them to life, will make them real, will make the characters understand the true nature of the shadows they face. You will eventually need some stats for those monsters, too—the rules in this chapter are here to help you create those stats quickly and easily, to give form to the horror with mechanical precision.

The player characters are the heroes. Monsters exist to illustrate what a dangerous, awful place Catachan can be—how it will remain if the heroes do not step in, how the darkness will consume all if they fail. You should not be rooting for the monsters to win, but they may challenge, and even sometimes defeat, the heroes, for the darkness is patient and the jungle is endless. If you feel like your monsters are being beaten too quickly, do not worry. Let the players revel in their victory, there is always more monsters, always more horrors, always more darkness. The jungle is always hungry, and it never forgets.

The principle of*think dangerous*sums up that philosophy—think of every monster like an arrow fired at the characters, a weapon of the shadows that craves their souls. The monsters are ammunition of the danger you are presenting, tools of the unending terror that defines existence on a Death World. Some may be smarter, faster, or more dangerous than others but until a monster warrants a name, a personality, or some other special consideration, it is an arrow. Engage in ranged combat and fire. Do not worry if you miss, for there are always more arrows, always more horrors, always more darkness.

A monster stops being mere ammunition when something in the world puts the spotlight on it, when it becomes something more than just another threat, when it becomes a true horror that the characters will remember. This might be a jungle lore roll that leads your description in an interesting direction or the result of your asking questions and building on the players' answers. Maybe the characters were overwhelmed in battle and had to run away, giving them a new-found fear and respect for the beast they fought, a horror that will haunt their dreams. When these things happen, feel free to give the monster a name and consider creating a danger to represent it, for named horrors are the ones that truly challenge their devotion.

One thing that your agenda and principles do not say anything about is setting up a fair fight. Heroes are often outnumbered or faced with ridiculous odds—sometimes they have to retreat and make a new plan, sometimes they must sacrifice to survive. Sometimes they suffer loss, sometimes they watch their comrades die, sometimes they must face the darkness alone. When adding a monster to a front, placing them in a mission area, or making them up on the fly your first responsibility is to the fiction (*portray a grim world*) and to give the characters a real threat (*make the characters heroes*), not to make a balanced fight. Death World: Catachan is not about balancing encounter levels or counting experience points; it is about survival and death-defying feats, about the endless struggle against the darkness, about faith and fury in the face of certain doom!

## Elements of a Monster

Every monster has**moves**that describe its behavior and abilities. Just like the normal GM moves, they’re things that you do when there’s a lull in the action or when the players give you a golden opportunity. As with other GM moves they can be hard or soft depending on the circumstances and the move: a move that’s irreversible and immediate is hard, a move that’s impending or easy to counter is soft.

Each monster has an**instinct**that describe its goals at a high level. Some monsters live for conquest, or treasure, or simply for blood. The monster’s instinct is the guide to how to use it in the fiction.

The monster’s**description**is where all its other features come from. The description is how you know what the monster really is, the other elements just reflect the description.

**Damage**is a measure of how much pain the monster can inflict at once. Just like player damage it’s a die to roll, maybe with some modifiers. A monster deals its damage to another monster or a player whenever it causes them physical harm.

Each monster has**tags**that describe how it deals damage, including the range(s) of its attacks. When trying to attack something out of its range (too close or too far) the monster’s out of luck, no damage. Any tag that can go on a weapon (like messy or slow) can also go on a monster.

There are special tags that apply only to monsters. These tags, listed below, describe the key attributes of the monster—qualities that describe how big they are and how, if at all, they organize themselves.

A monster’s**Wounds**is a measure of how much damage it can take before it dies. Just like players, when a monster takes damage it subtracts that amount from its Wounds. At 0 Wounds it’s dead, no last breath.

Some monsters are lucky enough to enjoy**armor**. It’s just like player armor: when a monster with armor takes damage it subtracts its armor from the damage done.

**Special qualities**describe innate aspects of the monster that are important to play. These are a guide to the fiction, and therefore the moves. A quality like intangible means just what it says: mundane stuff just passes through it. That means swinging a mundane sword at it isn't melee combat, for a start.

### Monsters Without Stats

Some creatures operate on a scale so far beyond the mortal that concepts like Wounds, armor, and damage just do not hold. Some creatures just aren’t dangerous in a fight. These creatures may still cause problems for the players and may even be defeated with clever thinking and enough preparation.

If a creature is of such a scale far beyond the players, or if it just doesn’t put up a physical fight, don’t assign it Wounds, damage, or armor. You can still use the monster creation rules to give it tags. The core of a stat-less monster is its instinct and moves; you can have it make its moves and act according to its instinct even without numeric stats.

## Monster Tags

*Warp-Touched*: It is by nature touched by the Warp or Chaos through and through.

*Devious*: Its main danger lies beyond the simple clash of battle.

*Amorphous*: Its anatomy and organs are bizarre and unnatural.

*Organized*: It has a group structure that aids it in survival. Defeating one may cause the wrath of others. One may sound an alarm.

*Intelligent*: It's smart enough that some individuals pick up other skills. The GM can adapt the monster by adding tags to reflect specific training, like a tech-priest or warrior.

*Hoarder*: It almost certainly has salvage or valuable items.

*Stealthy*: It can avoid detection and prefers to attack with the element of surprise.

*Terrifying*: Its presence and appearance evoke fear.

*Cautious*: It prizes survival over aggression.

*Construct*: It was made, not born

*Xenos*: It's from beyond this world

*Poisonous*: Its attacks deliver toxins or venoms.

*Regenerating*: It can heal damage quickly or regrow lost parts.

*Swarming*: It attacks in overwhelming numbers.

### Organization Tags

*Horde*: Where there’s one, there’s more. A lot more.

*Group*: Usually seen in small numbers, 3–6 or so.

*Solitary*: It lives and fights alone.

### Size Tags

*Tiny*: It’s much smaller than a halfling.

*Small*: It’s about halfling size.

*Large*: It’s much bigger than a human, about as big as a cart.

*Huge*: It’s as big as a small house or larger.

## Making Monsters

Monsters start with your description of them. Whether you’re making the monster before play or just as the players come face-to-face with it, every monster starts with a clear vision of what it is and what it does.

If you’re making a monster between sessions start by imagining it. Imagine what it looks like, what it does, why it stands out. Imagine the stories told about it and what effects it has had on the world.

If you’re making a monster on the fly during a session start by describing it to the players. Your description starts before the characters even lay eyes on it: describe where it lives, what marks it has made on the environment around it. Your description is the key to the monster.

When you find you need stats for the monster you use this series of questions to establish them. Answer every question based on the facts established and imagined. Don’t answer them aloud to anyone else, just note down the answers and the stats listed with each answer.

If two questions would grant the same tag don’t worry about it. If you like you can adjust damage or Wounds by 2 to reflect the tag that would be repeated, but it’s not necessary. If a combination of answers would reduce Wounds or damage below 1 they stay at 1.

When you’re finished your monster may have only one move. If this is the case and you plan on using the monster often, give it another 1–2 moves of your choice. These moves often describe secondary modes of attack, other uses for a primary mode of attack, or connections to a certain place in the world.

#### What is it known to do?

Write a monster move describing what it does.

#### What does it want that causes problems for others?

This is its instinct. Write it as an intended action.

#### How does it usually hunt or fight?

- In large groups: horde, d6 damage, 3 Wounds
- In small groups, about 2–5: group, d8 damage, 6 Wounds
- All by its lonesome: solitary, d10 damage, 12 Wounds

#### How big is it?

- Smaller than a house cat: tiny, hand, -2 damage
- Halfling-esque: small, close
- About human size: close
- As big as a cart: large, close, reach, +4 Wounds, +1 damage
- Much larger than a cart: huge, reach, +8 Wounds, +3 damage

#### What is its most important defense?

- Cloth or flesh: 0 armor
- Leathers or thick hide: 1 armor
- Flak or scales: 2 armor
- Carapace or chitin: 3 armor
- Permanent Warp protection: 4 armor, warp-touched

#### What is it known for? (Choose all that apply)

- Unrelenting strength: +2 damage, forceful
- Skill in offense: roll damage twice and take the better roll
- Skill in defense: +1 armor
- Deft strikes: +1 piercing
- Uncanny endurance: +4 Wounds
- Deceit and trickery: stealthy, write a move about dirty tricks
- A useful adaptation like being amphibious or having wings: Add a special quality for the adaptation
- The favor of the Warp: warp-touched, +2 damage or +2 Wounds or both (your call)
- Psyker powers: warp-touched, write a move about its psychic abilities
- Poisonous or venomous: poisonous, write a move about its toxins
- Regenerative abilities: regenerating, write a move about how it heals

#### What is its most common form of attack?

Note it along with the creature’s damage. Common answers include: a type of weapon, claws, a specific spell. Then answer these questions about it:

- Its armaments are vicious and obvious: +2 damage
- It lets the monster keep others at bay: reach
- Its armaments are small and weak: reduce its damage die size by one
- Its armaments can slice or pierce metal: messy, +1 piercing or +3 piercing if it can just tear metal apart
- Armor doesn’t help with the damage it deals (due to magic, size, etc.): ignores armor
- It usually attacks at range (with arrows, spells, or other projectiles): near or far or both (your call)

#### Which of these describe it? (Choose all that apply)

- It isn’t dangerous because of the wounds it inflicts, but for other reasons: devious, reduce its damage die size by one, write a move about why it’s dangerous
- It organizes into larger groups that it can call on for support: organized, write a move about calling on others for help
- It’s as smart as a human or thereabouts: intelligent
- It actively defends itself with a shield or similar: cautious, +1 Armor
- It collects trinkets that humans would consider valuable (gold, gems, secrets): hoarder
- It's from beyond this world: xenos, write a move about using its alien knowledge and power
- It’s kept alive by something beyond simple biology: +4 Wounds
- It was made by someone: construct, give it a special quality or two about its construction or purpose
- Its appearance is disturbing, terrible, or horrible: terrifying, write a special quality about why it’s so horrendous
- It doesn’t have organs or discernible anatomy: amorphous, +1 Armor, +3 Wounds
- It (or its species) is ancient—older than man, elves, and dwarves: increase its damage die size by one
- It abhors violence: roll damage twice and take the worst result

## Salvage and Loot

Monsters, much like soldiers, collect useful things. When the players search the belongings of a monster (be they on their person or tucked away somewhere) describe them honestly.

If the monster has accumulated some salvage you can roll that randomly. Start with the monster's damage die, modified if the monster is:

- Hoarder: roll damage die twice, take higher result
- Far from outpost: add at least one ration (usable by anyone with similar taste)
- Warp-touched: some strange item, possibly a relic
- Divine: a sign of the Emperor (or Chaos)
- Xenos: something not of this world
- Lord over others: +1d4 to the roll
- Ancient and noteworthy: +1d4 to the roll

Roll the monster's damage die plus any added dice to find the monster's salvage:

1. A few thrones worth of trade goods, 2d8 or so
2. An item useful to the current situation
3. Several thrones worth of goods, about 4d10
4. A small item (gem, art) of considerable value, worth as much as 2d10×10 thrones, 0 weight
5. Some minor relic or ancient technology
6. Useful information (in the form of data-slates, notes, etc.)
7. A bag of trade goods, 1d4×100 thrones or thereabouts. 1 weight per 100.
8. A very valuable small item (gem, art) worth 2d6×100 thrones, 0 weight
9. A chest of trade goods and other small valuables. 1 weight but worth 3d6×100 thrones.
10. A relic or ancient technology
11. Many bags of trade goods for a total of 2d4×100 thrones or so
12. A sign of office (medal, banner) worth at least 3d4×100 thrones
13. A large art item worth 4d4×100 thrones, 1 weight
14. A unique item worth at least 5d4×100 thrones
15. All the information needed to learn a new technique and roll again
16. A hidden path or secret route (or directions to one) and roll again
17. Something relating to one of the characters and roll again
18. A hoard: 1d10×1000 thrones and 1d10×10 gems worth 2d6×100 each

## Terrain

The monsters in this book are presented by*Terrain*. Terrain is a type of location and the native Catachan monsters you might find there. It's a way of grouping monsters by where they fit in the world. Terrain tells you what kind of native creatures might inhabit an area while your fronts tell you what monsters are working together or have ongoing plots.

Note that Chaos and Xenos Threats are their own categories, not terrain types. They represent external threats that have come to Catachan, rather than native creatures adapted to specific terrain.

When creating your own terrain types, they can be more specific. You could create terrain for the Northern Jungles or the Swamp Regions of Catachan.

Consult a terrain type to populate a front or when you want a threat that is only tangentially related to one of your fronts. For example, if the heroes are battling against the Ork Waaagh! front, by exploring the ancient ruins that the Orks have made their base then you might use monsters from The Ruins as a related threat—not truly part of the front but still a block in the heroes' path.

The monster stat blocks within each terrain type describe Wounds, damage, and all the other aspects of the monster. These monsters were created with the same process listed above, and the reasons for their stats are just as important as the stats themselves. Looking at the reasoning behind the stats will allow you to present the monsters honestly, answering questions that arise in Death World: Catachan like "can a pack of Catachan Devils wipe out an entire outpost?"

### The Jungles

The jungles of Catachan are home to some of the deadliest creatures in the galaxy. From the massive Catachan Devils that can swallow a man whole to the venom-snakes that kill with a single bite, every step in the jungle could be your last. Death-spiders spin webs of acid. Barking toads explode with toxic gas. Even the plants want you dead. In the deepest groves, trees that have stood since before man walked amidst their roots hide creatures old and powerful. Under the shadow of the ancient trees, things that shouldn't exist howl for blood. A word of caution, then, to those brave soldiers whose first foray into the jungle leads them into these deadly places; bad things live in the dark. Bad things with sharp teeth. The jungle is always hungry.

### The Swamps

All things give way to rot in the end. Food spoils on the table, men's minds go mad with age and disease. Even the world itself, when left untended and uncared for, can turn to black muck and stinking air. Things dwell in these parts of Catachan. Things gone just as bad as the swirling filth that fills the swamps. In these cesspit lowlands, soldiers will find such creatures as the deadly-eyed swamp predators or the famed, unkillable creatures that lurk beneath the murky waters. You'll need more than a dry pair of boots to survive these putrid fens. A lasgun would be a good start.

### The Caves

At the edges of civilization in the caves and tunnels below the old mountains of Catachan dwell all sorts of scheming, dangerous monsters. Some are wily and old, like the creatures that have adapted to the darkness. Others are strange aberrations of nature like the stinking, flesh-eating horrors. A word of caution, then, to those brave soldiers whose first foray into danger leads them into these dank and shadowy places; bad things live in the dark. Bad things with sharp teeth.

### The Ruins

Ruins dot the jungles of Catachan. Old bastions of long-forgotten Imperial outposts fallen to decay, to monsters, or to the whim of time. These ruins often cover a much more dangerous truth—underground complexes lousy with traps and monsters. Salvage, too. Which is why you're here. Why you're locked in mortal combat with creatures that have made these ruins their home. Battling massive creatures in caverns the size of whole countries. Maybe, though, you're the noble souls who've travelled to the world's heart to put an end to some ancient threat—the beast who, it is said, will one day consume all life and kill us all. We appreciate it, really. We'll all pray for you.

## Chaos

Chaos is not a terrain type, but a category of threat. The sermons of the Imperial Creed would tell you that the Emperor protects. They say that once you give yourself to the God-Emperor that all is order and duty and the golden light of Terra. Not so. Not for all. For some, after the Emperor's embrace loses its strength a darker power can take hold. The Warp rips the sane from their minds and gives them shambling madness full of hate and hunger. Chaos and corruption lend an ancient cult leader the power to live forever in the husk of a daemon-possessed body. There are bleak powers at play in shadowy corners all throughout Catachan. These creatures are the spawn of that fell corruption. Daemons walk the jungles. Cultists sacrifice in the name of dark gods. The Warp touches everything, and nothing is safe.

## Xenos Threats

Xenos Threats are not a terrain type, but a category of external threat. Sometimes, monsters do not come from Catachan at all. Beyond the void or below the deepest jungles, alien threats have come to this Death World. The Orks come in their Waaagh!, green-skinned berserkers who live only for war. No Ork travels alone. No slavering boy moves without his mob. You know that soon, the warboss will sound the call and the outposts will be besieged by the full fury of the Waaagh!. The Tyranids come in their swarms, the Great Devourer stripping worlds bare. Genestealers lurk in the shadows, infiltrating and corrupting. Carnifexes tear through everything in their path. You'll need more than a lasgun to survive these swarms. A heavy bolter would be a good start. And even then, you might not make it. The Eldar move like shadows through the trees, graceful and deadly. The Tau bring their strange technology and alien ways. The Necrons wait in ancient tombs, machines that will awaken and wreak their bloody havoc. You must be curious to know if these tales are true? What will you see when the alien threat reveals itself?
